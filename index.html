Miao Li

Ph.D. student in Machine Learning at the H. Milton Stewart School of Industrial and Systems Engineering, Georgia Tech.

I work on how language models generate, learn, and act as agents, toward efficient, self-improving AI. I work on how language models generate, learn, and act as agents, toward efficient, self-improving AI.

My research focuses on efficient LLM inference (particularly for diffusion language models), data-centric post-training, and agentic AI systems. I develop parallel decoding algorithms and end-to-end post-training pipelines that combine self-distillation-based data generation with learner-compatible supervision. I also develop agent harnesses that integrate tool orchestration, structured memory, and verifiable feedback to guide iterative policy discovery and ensure reliable execution.

My earlier research is in optimization and operations research: conformal prediction that certifies optimality gaps with finite-sample guarantees on large-scale economic dispatch, constraint-informed active learning for AC optimal power flow (ACOPF) optimization proxies, and sequential quadratic programming on Riemannian manifolds.

At Georgia Tech, I am advised by Pascal Van Hentenryck. Before that, I received an M.S. in Computational and Applied Mathematics from the University of Chicago and a B.S. in Mathematics from UC San Diego.

Research

Efficient Parallel Decoding Plan-Verify-Fill

  • Developed PVF, combining semantic-anchor planning and selective verification to reduce model forward passes by up to 65% versus confidence-based parallel decoding.

Data-Centric LLM Post-Training PACS

  • Built an end-to-end post-training pipeline spanning solution-hinted self-distillation, LLM-based quality filtering, and fine-tuning.
  • Extended distribution-compatible fine-tuning from answers to canvases, exploiting masked diffusion LLMs’ pre-specified, attention-visible canvas—a degree of freedom absent from standard autoregressive teacher forcing—to better match supervision to the model’s existing predictive distribution by adapting prediction conditions.
  • Developed PACS to select training contexts by model predictability—adapting how reference answers are taught while preserving their content.
  • Improved benchmark accuracy by up to 12.84 percentage points over vanilla SFT in evaluations spanning LLaDA-8B and Dream-7B.

LLM Interpretability Target-Token Asymmetry

  • Co-authored Target-Token Asymmetry: layer-ablation “depth specialization” can be a target-token artifact (41-condition audit); in GSM8K vs. Wikitext, removing whitespace flips its sign.

Evaluation-Guided Policy Discovery OPTAgent

  • Built an agent harness that evolves solver-orchestration policies through role-based search, policy memory, and verified evaluation feedback.
  • Compiled discovered policies into deterministic controllers with enforced compute budgets and no deployment-time LLM calls.
  • Increased mean feasible-instance count by 11% over a direct LLM agent under a shared workload budget.

Agent Benchmarking SkillsBench / BenchFlow

  • Contributed the gpu-cluster-online-scheduling task, a verifier-based benchmark module for evaluating agent behavior on online GPU-cluster scheduling under partial information.
  • Designed the task around operations-relevant constraints including GPU-type compatibility, capacity limits, CPU/memory feasibility, deadline penalties, fragmentation cost, and active-machine usage.
  • In task-level paired evaluation, the curated Skill improved a GPT-5.5-based agent’s pass rate from 20% to 40%, highlighting how domain-specific procedural guidance can improve agent performance beyond the base harness.

Optimality Assessment via Conformal Prediction CPUL-OMLT

  • Developed CPUL–OMLT, a lightweight, plug-and-play conformal framework compatible with any optimizer providing primal–dual bounds, delivering finite-sample coverage guarantees without modifying existing solvers.
  • Achieved up to 3.8× narrower optimality intervals at 90% target coverage on large-scale economic dispatch, enabling risk-aware stopping decisions and smarter compute allocation.
  • Applied the method to large-scale economic dispatch, a real-world operations research application where operators need auditable indicators of whether a solution is good enough for deployment.
  • Showed how narrower certified intervals can reduce unnecessary solver re-runs and manual review, saving deployment time and creating compute-allocation upside for harder optimization instances.

Structure-Aware Active Learning for Optimization Proxies

  • Developed constraint-guided active learning that combines learned active-set predictions with validation feedback to target hard, underrepresented solution regimes.
  • Reduced tail prediction errors on large-scale ACOPF benchmarks (up to 1,354 buses), reaching 90% of converged performance 2–4 rounds earlier and saving 600–1,200 expensive solver labels.

Riemannian Optimization SQP on manifolds

  • Conducted theoretical analysis of Sequential Quadratic Programming (SQP) algorithms for optimization on Riemannian manifolds (University of Chicago, Booth School of Business; preprint available upon request).
